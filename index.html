Part 01

Strength Training

01

Going to the gym is not progressing. If a year ago you were lifting the exact same weight as today, you haven't trained for a year. You have simply repeated the same month twelve times.

I

Always start with the core lifts

Muscle responds to tension, not to the feeling of having sweated. The heavy work goes first, while you're still fresh and entirely whole.

Apply it

Open every session with your most demanding lifts in the 6 to 10 rep range. Leave accessories and machines for the end.

II

Leave reps in reserve

Training to failure on everything takes more than it gives. You pile up systemic fatigue you can't recover from, and your performance drops the following week.

Apply it

Finish most working sets with one or two reps you could still physically complete. Only take the final set of an exercise to the absolute limit.

III

Write down every set

Without a precise record there is no progression. If you train from memory, you'll stall for months while falsely believing you're working hard.

Apply it

Track your exercise, sets, reps, and weight every single session. Aim to add a rep or a little load regularly.

IV

Complete your training blocks

Switching routines every three weeks is the fastest way to achieve zero progress. You never reach the phase where your body actually adapts.

Apply it

Run the exact same program for 8 to 10 weeks with a clear progression strategy before changing anything.

The consistency principle: four good training days repeated consistently for a year will always beat six perfect training days maintained for only three weeks. Choose a plan you can sustain on the days you don't feel like showing up.

Part 02

Zone 2 & Cardio

02

Cardiovascular fitness is the baseline of your endurance, heart health, and daily energy. It supports your strength work rather than working against it.

1

Frequency

2 to 3 sessions a week, depending on your level and how much priority you give it.

2 to 3 / week
2

Zone 2 priority

Keep most of your cardio at a pace where you can comfortably speak in short sentences without gasping.

Most volume
3

High intensity

Only 1 session a week, and only once you have a solid aerobic base built up.

Max 1 / week
4

Volume progression

Raise weekly volume gradually. Avoid massive jumps from one week to the next.

Gradual
5

The long session

1 longer session a week that stretches past your standard weekday duration.

1 / week
6

Daily steps

Aim for 7,000 to 10,000 as a general guideline, not a strict burden.

7k to 10k
7

Session ordering

Combining strength and cardio the same day, always do strength first.

Strength first
Mistakes to avoid

Going hard on every single session and never training at an easy, conversational pace. Failing to progress your weekly volume over time. Leaving zero recovery days between intense sessions.

Heart rate tracking

Use it as a helpful guide, not an absolute law. Formulas like 220 minus your age are generic estimates, so real world conversational testing is much more reliable.

Part 03

Fuel & Nutrition

03

Nutrition is your fastest lever to change how you look, perform, and recover day in and day out.

The numbers that matter
1.6–2.2 g/kg protein / day

Spread across 3 to 5 meals.

30–35 ml/kg water / day

Adjust for heat & sweat.

25–35 g fiber / day

Baseline for an active adult.

3–5 servings fruit + veg

Prioritize color variety.

Caloric direction: building or losing

Gaining muscle

Moderate surplus, roughly 10 to 20% above maintenance.

Losing fat

Moderate deficit, roughly 15 to 25% below maintenance.

In both cases, keep protein high. It's your primary tool for protecting muscle mass no matter which direction you're heading.
Nutrient timing & supplements
1

Pre workout, 1 to 3h before: carbs plus some protein, such as rice with chicken or oats with fruit.

2

Post workout: protein and carbs in the hours after. There's no magical 30 minute anabolic window, the real window is much wider.

3

Caffeine: 3 to 6 mg per kilo, taken 30 to 60 min before training. Cut it off 6 to 8h before bed even if you feel like you'll fall asleep fine.

4

Electrolytes: useful during long workouts, heavy sweating, or high heat. Sodium is the key mineral to watch.

Worth it vs skip it

The golden rule: no timing strategy can ever make up for a messy diet the rest of the day. Your total daily intake matters far more than when you eat it.

Part 04

Rest & Recovery

04

Master the fundamentals of recovery before you spend a single dime on fancy tools or gadgets.

The recovery hierarchy, biggest impact first
1

Sleep, 7 to 9h

Regular schedules, morning sunlight exposure, and cutting caffeine 6 to 8 hours before bed.

2

Nutrition

Sufficient protein and real food every single day.

3

Training management

Smart progression paired with deload weeks when fatigue accumulates.

4

Stress & rest

Real days off and screen free breaks.

The extra 5%, optional tools
Sauna

Helps relaxation and perceived recovery. Evidence on performance is mixed.

Cold plunge

May cut perceived soreness short term. Right after lifting it can blunt adaptations.

Compression boots

Mostly improve how recovered you feel. Limited evidence on real performance.

Red light

Evidence still preliminary. Nothing solid backing it yet.

Simple rule

If you do not sleep, eat well, or train with clear intent, no recovery gadget on earth is going to fix it.

Recovery, the night protocol

Wind down, in this order

This is a sequence, not a schedule. Run through these five steps in order, whatever time works for your life.

Step 1

Dim the lights

Warm and low lighting. Avoid blasting overhead ceiling lights right up until bedtime.

Step 2

Shower & teeth

Get clean and leave tomorrow's clothes ready to eliminate a decision in the morning.

Step 3

Phone out of room

If you use your phone as an alarm, buy a cheap alarm clock instead.

Step 4

Reading

Spend 20 minutes reading physical paper books to signal to your body that the day is over.

Step 5

Lights out

Cold and dark room at the exact same time every night, weekends included.

What ruins the night

Drinking caffeine after 2pm, alcohol during the week, eating right before bed, and training heavy past 9pm. Sleeping 5 hours during the week thinking you'll catch up on Saturday is a myth. You cannot recover lost sleep.

Part 05

Adaptability Rules

05

Things are going to break. The difference is not about avoiding it, but what you choose to do the next day. This separates those who last for months from those who quit in weeks.

If you travel

Do a quick room session with pushups, Bulgarian split squats, chair dips, and planks for 4 rounds. Prioritize steps and find good food before you get desperate.

If you dine out

Order meat or fish with vegetables, drink water, and enjoy your meal. What ruins people isn't a single dinner, but skipping breakfast the next day to compensate.

If a day goes sideways

Do not try to overcompensate. Just return to your normal routine the next day. One bad day does not break a week, but two in a row do.

If you sleep badly

Do not load up on extra caffeine or force a brutal workout. Lower your intensity, hit your steps, and go to bed 30 minutes earlier.

If you have no motivation

Show up anyway and do half the work. You'll end up finishing the whole session 80 percent of the time, and the other 20 percent you kept the habit alive.

The four of five rule: if you hit four days out of every five, you're on track. Chasing 100 percent perfection is the fastest way to quit at your first stumble.
